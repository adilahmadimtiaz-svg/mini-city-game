How MINI QPR Was Made

MINI QPR started with a simple thought: big games are set in big fictional cities, but nobody had ever made a game about Qadirpur Rawan — a small, real town near Multan, Pakistan. So Adil, a student developer who lives there, decided to build one himself. The result is a free, GTA-style open-world game that runs in a web browser and recreates his own town: its main road, its landmarks, its rickshaws, its market and its everyday life.

The finished game: the hero — Adil, in a white suit and black shades — standing on a QPR street with Mission 1 already waiting.

Building a real town, not a fictional city

Most open-world games invent their map. MINI QPR does the opposite. Khanewal Road, the real road that cuts through Qadirpur Rawan, runs diagonally across the game map exactly as it does in real life. Around it sit the places every local knows: the Passport Office, Zaheer Hospital and Trauma Center, the Govt Degree College and the Associate College, Al Haadi Foods, Dubai Garments & School Uniforms, Nandhla Cloth House, Pizza Click, Sultan Bahoo Electronics, the Sabzi Mandi fruit and vegetable market, the Jamia Masjid Muhammadia Salfia and the QPR Supermarket — surrounded by the mohalla houses where the town actually lives.

One file, a full 3D world

Technically, the whole game lives in a single web page built with Three.js, the JavaScript 3D library. There is nothing to install: the town, the vehicles, the people and the missions all load in the browser, on a phone or a computer. Cars, motorbikes, auto rickshaws, a helicopter and an airplane can all be driven or flown. The town is alive in small ways that locals recognise — the morning milkman on his bicycle, school children, cows, dogs, cats and birds, and shoppers at the supermarket.

The look, the sound, the polish

The game went through several rebuilds. The city layout and the cars were reworked until the town felt like a real place rather than a cartoon, and the hero was redesigned — he no longer uses a photo face; he wears a white suit and keeps his black shades. Later updates added full sound with Web Audio (press M to mute), a wanted-level police system, a working shop inside the supermarket, and touch controls with an on-screen joystick for phones.

Launch

MINI QPR was published on 3 November 2026 on GitHub Pages and itch.io, and released as an Android app on APKPure on 5 November 2026. It is completely free. For its developer, the best review is the simplest one: people from Qadirpur Rawan opening the game and recognising their own streets.
